A guide explains, often for the first time, what lessons cannot explain well: a rule across many cases, the register of a word, the way fast speech changes forms, a table that shows a whole system at once. What it teaches counts as taught from its position, like a lesson's.

1. State the rule in one line the learner can keep.
2. Lay out the cases in parts, each with a heading, a short explanation and examples. Build on what earlier lessons showed, and add what they could not show.
3. Where a table shows the rule best, use a table.
4. A rule with exceptions says "most" or "usually", above all where the unit's own sentences show the exception.
5. End with three checks that ask the learner to apply the rule.
