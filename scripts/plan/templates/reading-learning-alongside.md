In this kind of reading a character learns something alongside the reader. A visitor, a newcomer or a child asks what a learner would ask, and someone who knows answers.

1. Keep the whole text in the language as native speakers use it. The one who asks is curious, never a model of mistakes.
2. Let the explanation come out in conversation: a question, an answer, a doubt, an example.
3. Every fact a character states is checked against a source before it is written.
4. Use the patterns the plan requires, teach only the table words the plan lists, and keep to the length of one part.
5. Write comprehension questions on what was explained and on who thought what.
