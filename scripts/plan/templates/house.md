These rules hold for every module of every course.

- **A strong foundation for what comes next.** Words and patterns are taught so that every later lesson has a strong foundation to build on. Each new item is met through what the learner already owns, is chosen early for how much later material leans on it, and is soon used as support for the next new items. A module that passes the checker but teaches words in isolation still fails.
- **Taught before used.** The check that follows from it: use only words and patterns the learner knows by now (listed below) and what this module teaches. Review is built in: reuse what the last modules taught, the words due for reuse first.
- **Words are taught where they are first used.** A new word gets a vocabulary card in the module that first uses it, and is first met in a sentence made of known words. A form of a word already taught gets a gloss, never a second card.
- **This module's words are its only new cards.** If the module needs a word the plan does not give it, say so in your notes and do not add the card.
- **Real situations.** Open and close in the situation the plan names, with the unit's cast and places. Keep each character's age, look and relations as the cast list gives them.
- **Framed prompts.** After the first unit, no bare sentence to translate, and no translation in disguise: never "Tell Maya: <an English sentence>". Every item has a speaker, a situation and a reason; describe what the speaker wants, and give a French cue where the item needs one. No bare drill such as "Put it in the past.": say who is talking and why.
- **One world.** Every module of a unit agrees with the unit's cast facts (who likes what, who lives where, which floor, where each place is, what time it opens). Check them before you write a line about a character or a place.
- **Answers.** An accept list holds every natural answer a learner would give, not only the one the module models: other word orders, the future where it fits, the short spoken forms in a friend's mouth, a name instead of a pronoun. A listed mistake is wrong French; when it is French with another meaning, its feedback says what it means.
- **Size.** Stay inside the sizes below. A module that cannot fit is reported, not crammed.
- **Natural, correct language.** Write what a native speaker would say in that situation, at that age and in that place. Remove any line no speaker would say, however correct it is. A rule with exceptions says "most" or "usually", and so does a rule that the unit's own sentences break.
- **The country is part of the course.** Where the plan gives a culture note, the module carries it in two lines beside the word it belongs to. Check every historical fact against a source before you write it, and leave out what you cannot confirm.
- **How people talk.** Where the plan lists spoken words for this module, its dialogue uses them, in the right mouths: young people with friends, not an old neighbour or a shopkeeper. A speaker from another region or country keeps their own variety. Slang marked for recognition is understood by the learner and never asked for.
- **Readings come after the lessons in story time.** A unit's reading happens after what its lessons showed, or alongside it, never before it: it can look back on the lessons, and it recombines them.
- **No images.** Readings are written as text. Record a character's look in words only.
