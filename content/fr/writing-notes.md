# Writing the French course: notes for this language

These notes go into every writer brief for French, after the method that holds for every language.

## The world of the course

- Units 13 to 15 and 22 to 29 share one building in the Croix-Rousse, a hillside district of Lyon: Léa and her brother Noé on the third floor, Mme Garnier, a retired teacher, on the ground floor, and Karim's bakery on the corner.
- Units 16 to 21 keep the casts of the earlier readings in their own towns: Amélie and her family in Normandy, Sophie in Bordeaux, Marc and Pierre in Paris.
- Units 22 to 29 follow Élodie Renard through her first year in Lyon. She is 28, blonde, a civil engineer from Lille. Her twin brother is Romain Renard, a hairdresser in Lille; his children are Lina and Adam. Her boyfriend, Oliver Hughes, is a history teacher from Bath.
- A lead is French, born and raised in France, with a first name a beginner reads as French. The people around the lead show France as it is.
- A visitor such as Oliver or Maya may ask what a learner would ask. The French on the page stays native French.

## French in this course

- Dialogue between friends, family and young people is spoken French: it may drop *ne*, use *on* for *nous* and use the small words of speech the plan lists for the module. Narration, letters and articles keep written French.
- Slang and verlan are for recognition. A learner is asked what *meuf* or *relou* means and who would say it, and is never asked to produce it. Nothing vulgar or insulting appears.
- When a situation does not say who is spoken to, items accept both *tu* and *vous*. When it does not fix the question form, items accept intonation, *est-ce que* and inversion. When the speaker's gender is not fixed, items accept both agreements.
- History is told in the *passé composé* and the *imparfait*. The *passé simple* waits for the B2 units.
- France is part of every unit: its history, ancient history included, and its customs. The plan names what each unit carries.
