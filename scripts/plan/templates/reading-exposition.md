An exposition explains how or why something is so, in plain explanatory prose with no characters and no plot.

1. State what is being explained, then build the explanation step by step, each step resting on the one before.
2. Every fact is checked against a source before it is written.
3. Use the connectors and patterns the plan requires; this is the language of guidebooks, newspapers and reading exams.
4. Teach only the table words the plan lists and keep to the length of one part.
5. Write comprehension questions on the claims the text makes and the grounds it gives.
